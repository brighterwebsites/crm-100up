 six things, roughly in order of how expensive they are to get wrong.

1. The commercial and IP conversation, before another line of code. This is the one I'd genuinely push on. What you're building isn't a website with a CRM bolted on — it's a vertical SaaS for solar installers: quoting engine, STC rebate logic, CEC compliance output, stock-to-install consumption, PO lifecycle. There are a few hundred solar installers in Victoria with the same problem. If you're billing Fred hourly for a custom build, you're funding an asset you don't own, and the value-based pricing conversation never happens. Settle now: is this Fred's bespoke system, a licensed product he's the first customer of, or a joint venture? Get it in writing before Phase B, because the answer also determines the technical decision in Theme A — if it's ever going to serve a second installer, the configurable product catalogue (Level 3) stops being optional and becomes the foundation. Deciding that after you've built the calculators is a rewrite. |  No Fred owns this - all his logic, im just taking what he has designed and making it functional and logical in the cloud. 

2. He's still iterating on V46, and that's a moving target. Every improvement he makes to the HTML is something you either re-implement or lose. You need an explicit agreement, not a vague one: from an agreed date the old file is frozen except for genuine bug fixes, and new ideas go on the roadmap list instead. If he won't freeze it, that's fine — but then the rebuild scope is uncapped and the commercial model has to reflect that. Related: the version numbering has already drifted (filename says V46, the in-app title says V3, the calculator badge says v78, the CRM badge says V17), so you can't currently tell from a filename which build he's on.

3. Collect the artefacts during the meeting, not after. His browser localStorage is the only copy of live data — if he clears his cache it's gone. Get, while you're sitting there: his current HTML file, a fresh CRM JSON export, and a fresh assumptions export. The copies in the repo are from 25 June and 20 June.

4. Let him drive the cloud app for twenty minutes. Nobody has actually used it. Every "complete" in my status table is complete against the old file's behaviour, not against Fred's judgement. Twenty minutes of him clicking through the pipeline and stock screens will produce more real requirements than the 37 questions will. Worth confirming the deployed URL loads before you screen-share — the app is a Cloudflare Workers static deploy under the project name crm-100up, and the build needs VITE_SUPABASE_URL and VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY set as Cloudflare build variables, so it's worth a quick check that the deployed build actually connects.

5. I'd soften what I said about the import script. Having looked at the volumes again — 18 jobs, 17 stock items, 3 suppliers, 1 receipt — re-keying that by hand is a few hours. Fixing and testing the import script against the current schema, including customer deduplication, is plausibly the same or more, and carries the risk of a silently wrong import. Worth putting to Fred as a straight choice rather than assuming we fix the script. If he's happy to re-key, you delete a blocker and a maintenance liability in one move.

6. Accounts, billing and the Xero boundary. Whose Supabase project and Cloudflare account is this on, who pays the running costs, and what happens to the data if the engagement ends? There's real customer PII in there now, so it needs an answer. Separately: "Quote in Xero" is already a pipeline step, so Xero is in the process and holds contacts, quotes and invoices. The CRM now has its own customer records. Decide which system owns the customer, or you'll be maintaining two lists inside six months.

One smaller thing worth asking while you're there: does anyone other than Fred touch this — a partner, a bookkeeper, subcontract installers? It changes the roles model, and it's much cheaper to know now.
